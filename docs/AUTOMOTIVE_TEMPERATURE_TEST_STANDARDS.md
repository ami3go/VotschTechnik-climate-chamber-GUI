# Automotive DUT temperature-test standards — international, OEM, component and EV

> **Research snapshot:** 2026-11-21. **Scope:** 86 catalogued standards/specifications, 16 LV 124 climatic/lifetime method IDs, and 10 environmental test families. This is a planning/reference index, **not** a substitute for the normative standard, an OEM-controlled test plan, or a certificate of compliance.

## Purpose and interpretation

This catalogue supports planning and documenting automotive environmental tests for ECUs, sensors, actuators, electronic assemblies, lighting, connectors, harnesses, semiconductors, trim, traction batteries and REESS. The existence of a temperature test in a standard **does not mean every device must run it**; the product specification, vehicle program, installation location, grade, customer agreement, standard revision, conditioning sequence and acceptance criteria determine what is mandatory.

**Normative vs. method vs. catalogue:** ISO/IEC/JEDEC methods define possible test procedures; qualification requirements may invoke them. Some OEM standards are controlled or unavailable publicly. Laboratory capability listings prove that a laboratory offers a named method, **not** the exact OEM clause, revision, temperature setpoint or acceptance criterion. Do not manufacture numeric limits from a public abstract or a lab catalogue.

**Source-confidence key:** **A** = issuer, publisher, government/legal text or base standard; **B** = accredited laboratory scope or established laboratory technical matrix; **C** = supplier or laboratory listing/qualification service; **D** = secondary reseller/description. `A/B` combines sources. Confidence describes the **identification and broad test family**, not unrestricted access to the complete operative text.

**Safety/operational limitations:** A conventional single-zone chamber may implement slow/controlled ramps and soaks; thermal shock, water splash/immersion, solar radiation, active power cycling and high-temperature semiconductor stresses can require dedicated fixtures and safety systems. Confirm chamber capability, DUT limits, power supplies, interlocks and operator approval before execution.

## Legal/normative example: UN R100 Annex 9B REESS temperature cycling

The publicly accessible 2026 consolidated Regulation No. 100 provides a **specific REESS qualification test**: at least five cycles alternating +60 ±2 °C and −40 ±2 °C, with at least six hours at each extreme and no more than 30 minutes transfer; then recovery for 24 hours at 22 ±5 °C and the prescribed follow-up checks. **Do not reuse this as a generic automotive ECU profile**. Use the legally applicable regulation series, vehicle approval jurisdiction, battery configuration and current official annex text. See the UN R100 source in the Battery EV table.

## Implementation notes for the Vötsch chamber GUI

1. **Store the method reference, not a hardcoded compliance claim.** A future profile template can carry issuer, standard, revision, clause, target product, customer, approval state and source reference. Do not label a generic ramp/soak profile as certified to a standard.
2. **Keep test parameters explicit.** Temperatures, dwell times, number of cycles, ramp/transfer tolerance, DUT power state, allowed interruptions, recovery time and acceptance limits must come from a controlled profile, not inferred from this index.
3. **Validate chamber capabilities.** Distinguish chamber-air setpoint from measured DUT temperature; check actual temperature/humidity bounds, ramp rate under load, stability, humidity/dew-point envelope and external fixture requirements. Never substitute a single-zone ramp for a two-zone thermal-shock method without an approved equivalence.
4. **Use the existing Python chamber driver as the only instrument-communication route.** The NiceGUI layer should call the `VotschTechnik-climate-chamber-Python` driver, not open alternate raw network/control connections.
5. **Integrate DUT monitoring.** Powered test plans should define measurement/telemetry sampling, functional checkpoints, reconnect policy, limit evaluation, synchronized timestamps, event logging and retention of raw chamber and DUT evidence.
6. **Watchdog long runs and responses.** For multi-hour or multi-day exposure, use an independent heartbeat/watchdog with timeout, persistent state, resume/reconciliation rules, stalled-measurement detection and safe abort; do not claim completion from a UI timer. Timeouts must be configurable in the approved test profile.
7. **Preserve the authorization boundary.** Offline simulation/emulation requires no physical-bench authorization; real energized chamber/DUT testing follows the repository HIL approval procedure and installed safety interlocks.
8. **Maintain provenance.** Record the exact issue/version of each standard, effective date, purchased/controlled document custodian, approved deviation and report template; revalidate this snapshot before customer use.

## Research gaps and follow-up checks

- **OEM-controlled revisions and clause numbers:** Obtain current VW 80000/LV 124, BMW GS 95024-3-1, MBN 10306, GMW3172, Ford CETP, Stellantis CS.00056, Renault and Japanese OEM texts under the project/customer license. Match every planned profile to its precise revision and clause.
- **Regional/historical references:** Check current validity of QC/T 413-2002, JIS D 0204, ETSI EN 300 019-2-5 applicability, ISO 19453-4:2018 (withdrawn) and other older documents in the project regulatory matrix.
- **Lab scope vs. requirements:** A2LA/SGS/IABG/TÜV/other laboratory service listings do not validate exact numeric limits, duration, acceptance criteria, sample quantity, mandatory status or current applicability.
- **Product-based exclusions:** Many material, cable, semiconductor and traction-battery standards are not applicable to a general climate-chamber ECU test and may require dedicated fixtures, burn-in or abuse-test equipment.
- **Coverage is curated, not universal:** Automotive OEM standards and supplier-specific customer specifications are frequently proprietary or variant-specific. This index enumerates the 86 catalogued sources, not every worldwide specification.
